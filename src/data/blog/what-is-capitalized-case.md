Capitalized case is a simple formatting style in which the first letter of every word is uppercase and the remaining letters are lowercase. For example, `a QUICK guide TO text formatting` becomes `A Quick Guide To Text Formatting`.

It is useful when you need a consistent, easy-to-scan format for labels, names, short phrases, and imported data. Use the [Capitalized Case Converter](/capitalized-case) to apply it instantly.

## What does capitalized case look like?

Here are a few examples:

| Original text | Capitalized case |
| --- | --- |
| `hello world` | `Hello World` |
| `CUSTOMER SUPPORT TEAM` | `Customer Support Team` |
| `my new project name` | `My New Project Name` |
| `a quick guide to writing` | `A Quick Guide To Writing` |

The key rule is simple: every word starts with a capital letter. This makes capitalized case predictable, but it is not always the same as editorial title case.

## Capitalized case vs. title case

Capitalized case capitalizes every word. Title case follows grammatical rules and often keeps short articles, conjunctions, and prepositions lowercase in the middle of a title.

For example:

- Capitalized case: `How To Write A Better Blog Post`
- Title case: `How to Write a Better Blog Post`

Use [Title Case](/title-case) for headlines that need standard editorial styling. Use capitalized case when every word should follow the same pattern, such as a cleaned-up list of names or labels.

## When should you use capitalized case?

Capitalized case can be useful for:

- Names in a spreadsheet or customer list
- Navigation labels and menu items
- Product or category labels
- Short display names imported from inconsistent sources
- Simple presentation headings where a strict editorial style is not required

For body copy, [sentence case](/sentence-case) is easier to read. For all-uppercase data, start with the [Capitalized Case Converter](/capitalized-case) and then review names, acronyms, and brand terms.

## Common mistakes to avoid

### Using capitalized case for a full paragraph

Capitalizing every word can feel distracting in long content. Use sentence case for paragraphs and reserve capitalized case for short labels, names, or display text.

### Confusing it with title case

Title case is generally better for an article, book, or presentation heading because it handles small words more naturally. Read our guide on [how to capitalize a title correctly](/blog/how-to-capitalize-a-title-correctly) when you are choosing between the two.

### Forgetting acronyms and brand names

An automatic converter may turn `API` into `Api` or preserve a brand in a way that does not match its official styling. Proofread important text and correct specialized terms manually.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is capitalized case the same as title case?

No. Capitalized case capitalizes every word. Title case follows additional rules that usually keep small words such as `to`, `and`, and `of` lowercase when they appear in the middle of a title.

### Can I use capitalized case for names?

Yes. It is helpful for standardizing a list of names, but review names with prefixes, apostrophes, hyphens, or unusual brand-specific capitalization.
